## Business Recommendations

### Recommendation 1 - Incentivize Contract Upgrades
**Priority: HIGH**

The 18x churn difference between month-to-month and two-year contracts is the clearest signal in the data. The business should immediately launch a campaign offering month-to-month customers a discount to upgrade to annual or two-year contracts. Even converting 20% of high-risk month-to-month customers could significantly reduce overall churn.

**Target:** 3,610 month-to-month customers currently churning at 45.8%

---

### Recommendation 2 - Build a 90-Day Onboarding Program
**Priority: HIGH**

With 53.3% of customers churning in the first 6 months, the company needs a structured early-tenure retention program. This should include proactive outreach at 30/60/90 days, onboarding guides, and exclusive first-year discounts to get customers through the critical early window.

**Target:** All new customers in their first 6 months

---

### Recommendation 3 - Bundle Premium Tech Support
**Priority: HIGH**

Customers with Premium Tech Support churn at nearly 3x lower rates (15.2% vs 41.6%). The company should bundle tech support into new customer packages - either free for the first year or heavily discounted. The retention value far outweighs the cost of providing support.

**Target:** 3,473 customers currently without Premium Tech Support

---

### Recommendation 4 - Address the Fiber Optic Churn Problem
**Priority: HIGH**

Fiber Optic customers churn at 40.7% - likely due to competitors offering better speeds or pricing. The business should audit its Fiber Optic pricing against competitors and offer a loyalty discount for Fiber Optic customers with less than 12 months tenure.

**Target:** 3,035 Fiber Optic customers

---

### Recommendation 5 - Stop Spending on Offer E
**Priority: MEDIUM**

Offer E has the highest churn rate of any promotional offer. Marketing budget should be reallocated toward Offer A and Offer B which are clearly driving better long-term retention.

**Target:** Marketing team / promotional budget

---

### Recommendation 6 - Focus Retention on Southern California
**Priority: MEDIUM**

San Diego (64.9%), Fallbrook (60.5%), and Temecula (57.9%) have dramatically higher churn rates than the state average (26.5%). The company should deploy targeted local retention campaigns and consider region-specific pricing in these zip codes.

**Target:** Southern California customer base

---

### Recommendation 7 - Incentivize Digital Payment Enrollment
**Priority: LOW**

Credit Card customers churn at 16.5% vs 41.6% for Mailed Check customers. The company should incentivize customers to switch to automatic digital payments through a small monthly discount - this would reduce churn risk and improve cash flow predictability.

**Target:** Customers currently paying by Mailed Check

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Contract Type is the Strongest Churn Predictor
Month-to-month customers churn at **45.8%** - 18x the rate of two-year contract customers (2.5%). This is the single most impactful variable in the dataset.

| Contract Type | Churn Rate |
|--------------|------------|
| Month-to-Month | 45.8% |
| One Year | 10.7% |
| Two Year | 2.5% |

---

### 2. Early Tenure Customers Are at Extreme Risk
Customers in their first 6 months churn at **53.3%**. This drops steadily with tenure - customers who stay beyond 4 years churn at only 9.5%. The first 6 months is the most critical retention window.

| Tenure Group | Churn Rate |
|-------------|------------|
| 0–6 months | 53.3% |
| 7–12 months | 35.3% |
| 13–24 months | 28.7% |
| 25–48 months | 20.4% |
| 48+ months | 9.5% |

---

### 3. Fiber Optic Has a Serious Churn Problem
Fiber Optic customers churn at **40.7%** - more than double the DSL rate (18.6%). Given that Fiber Optic is typically the highest-revenue product, this represents a disproportionate share of revenue loss.

| Internet Type | Churn Rate |
|--------------|------------|
| Fiber Optic | 40.7% |
| Cable | 25.7% |
| DSL | 18.6% |
| No Internet | 7.4% |

---

### 4. Premium Tech Support is a Powerful Retention Tool
Customers without Premium Tech Support churn at **41.6%** vs just **15.2%** for those with it - nearly a 3x difference. Customers who feel supported are significantly more loyal.

---

### 5. Competitor Pressure is the #1 Churn Driver
**33.3%** of all churned customers left because a competitor offered better devices or a better deal - the leading churn category by a wide margin.

| Churn Reason | % of Churned |
|-------------|--------------|
| Competitor had better devices | 16.7% |
| Competitor made better offer | 16.6% |
| Attitude of support person | 11.8% |
| Don't know | 7.0% |
| Competitor offered more data | 6.3% |

---

### 6. High-Value Customers Are Leaving
Churned customers paid an average of **$73.35/month** vs **$61.74/month** for retained customers - an $11.61 difference. The company is disproportionately losing its highest-paying customers.

---

### 7. $3.68M in Total Revenue Lost
The company lost **$3.68M in total revenue** from churned customers with **$137K in monthly recurring revenue** at risk. Competitor-driven churn accounts for the largest share of this loss.

---

### 8. San Diego is the Highest-Risk Region
San Diego has a **64.9% churn rate** with 185 churned customers. Fallbrook (60.5%) and Temecula (57.9%) follow closely. Southern California is clearly the most at-risk region in the state.

| City | Churn Rate | Customers Churned |
|------|------------|-------------------|
| San Diego | 64.9% | 185 |
| Fallbrook | 60.5% | 26 |
| Temecula | 57.9% | 22 |
| Los Angeles | 26.6% | 78 |
| San Francisco | 29.8% | 31 |

---

### 9. Payment Method Correlates with Loyalty
Customers paying by Mailed Check churn at **41.6%** while Credit Card customers churn at only **16.5%**. Digital payment customers are significantly more engaged and loyal.

---

### 10. Offer E is Failing as a Retention Tool
Customers who received **Offer E have the highest churn rate** of any promotional offer. Offer A and Offer B customers churn at significantly lower rates, suggesting these promotions are more effective at driving long-term loyalty.
